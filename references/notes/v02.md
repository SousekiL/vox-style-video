# v02: Rome collage diorama (continuous generative push-in)

Source: `references/videos/v02_1120129738625508713.mp4`. Work files and scripts: `references/analysis/v02/work/` (`bbox.py`, `cam*.py`, `seg.py`, `region.py`, `strip.py`, `fit.py`; outputs `bbox.txt`, `cam_fg*.txt`, `s_*.txt`, `st_*.png`).

## 1. Summary
This is a 10 s single-shot "Rome" postcard collage: one compact cluster of cut-out photos sits in the middle of a flat warm off-white page. The photos are a Colosseum, a cypress, a Fiat 500, a peacock, a Pulcinella figure, a traffic light, a beach ball, an antique column/arch and handwritten paper scraps. Flat geometric paper shapes are mixed in: an ochre sun disc, a red triangle, a red rectangle, a teal quad and an orange chevron. Thin ink connector lines with small open circles and dots sit on top. Nothing ever cuts. The camera pushes in slowly and continuously (about 1.43x over 10 s) while objects drive, turn in 3D, morph and swap identity by short dissolves. **The motion is almost certainly an image-to-video AI generation, not keyframed cut-outs.** The evidence: flat cut-outs rotate in 3D (car f150–f175), objects turn into other objects (peacock → column base f196–f199), shapes dissolve into gradients (triangle f163–f241), and two 1-frame "jump" swaps occur (f113, f197). The look comes from three things together: a museum-catalogue collage on a large empty page, a muted warm grade with exactly three saturated accents (ochre, brick red, teal), and smooth, unstepped 24 fps drift.

## 5. Typography
- **No set type.** The only "text" is illegible handwritten cursive in brown ink on parchment scraps. It works as texture, not copy: the notes behind the triangle measure about 12 × 10 % of the frame, and the ink strokes are about 1 px native, roughly 0.25 % of frame height.
- If you need text in this style, use a cursive script as texture only: `Homemade Apple` or `La Belle Aurore` at about 1.5–2 % frame height, colour `#5a4630` at 70 % opacity, on a parchment rectangle `#d4c6b3`. Keep it illegible-small. Any real headline would have to come from another reference.

## 6. Layout and composition
- **One central cluster ("diorama island")**, never edge-to-edge. Bbox: f1 50 × 54 % of the frame centred at (50.5, 52.4); f65 69 × 63 %; f241 64 × 76 % centred at (48.6, 53.9). The margin to the frame edge stays ≥ 8 % on the left/right and ≥ 6 % at top/bottom the whole time.
- **Ground plane:** a feathered cobblestone ellipse/patch under the objects (y about 68–88 %). Its edge fades out over about 3–5 % of the frame. It anchors the collage like a museum plinth.
- **Depth layers: 5–6.** From back to front: paper scraps and flat shapes (disc, rectangle, triangle) → architecture (Colosseum, arch) → mid props (cypress, traffic light, figure) → hero object (car or peacock) → foreground props (ball, chevron) → ink connector lines and dots on top.
- **Overlap rule:** heavy overlap, so each object hides 20–50 % of the one behind. Flat shapes always sit *behind* photo cut-outs. The sun disc peeks out behind the Colosseum's top-right, about 40 % visible.
- **Framing devices:** thin ink line from a small dot to an open circle (circle Ø ≈ 3 % W ≈ 5.3 % H, stroke about 1 px native). Loose curved hairlines loop around the base. Small solid dots: black Ø ≈ 1.5 % H; red Ø ≈ 2 % H.
- **Hero rotation:** the hero changes every 2–3 s (car → peacock → figure removed → car → arch/chevron → car). The cluster's visual centre of mass moves only ±3 % W (cx 45.5–52.1 %).

## 8. Motion vocabulary
All motion is smooth at 24 fps (no stepping, no boil). Easings below are least-squares fits of `cubic-bezier` to normalised progress (`work/fit.py`; rms in progress units).

1. **Continuous push-in (camera dolly)**
   - Global scale, from SIFT/ORB similarity transforms on foreground features: 1.00 (f1) → about 1.00–1.04 (f49) → 1.16 (f121) → 1.30 (f169) → 1.43 (f241).
   - Rate: about 0 %/frame until f49, then **0.25–0.29 %/frame (6–7 %/s) for f61–f169**, then about 0.13 %/frame (3 %/s) for f169–f241. It never settles; it is still moving on the last frame.
   - Zoom anchor (fixed point) is about (29 %, 54 %) of the frame during the main push, drifting to about (12–15 %, 58 %) late. The push is off-centre, toward the lower-left.
   - Rotation is ≤ 0.5° (noise).
   - Whole-shot fit: `cubic-bezier(0.45,-0.10,0.45,0.60)` (rms 0.010), i.e. slow-in then near-linear with a mild late slowdown.
   - Cross-check: red triangle base width 5.70 → 6.42 % W from f1 to f115 (×1.13) vs camera ×1.14.
2. **Hero slide (car drives left and toward camera)**: f1–f65, 64 f = 2.67 s.
   - Front bumper x: 37.0, 34.5, 31.5, 28.0, 24.3, 20.6, 17.8, 15.4, 13.0, 10.7, 9.3, 8.9 % (every 6 f).
   - Peak speed 0.62 %W/frame (15 %W/s) at f19–f31. It starts already moving (0.42 %/f at f1).
   - **Ease `cubic-bezier(0.25,0.20,0.60,0.90)`** (rms 0.007; linear rms 0.076).
   - Car height grows 24 → 29 % H (×1.2) while the camera is at ×1.02, so the object approaches the lens. No overshoot.
3. **Slide-stop right**: car f137–f157, 20 f = 0.83 s.
   - Right edge 82.4 → 90.4 % W; v0 ≈ 0.85 %W/f decaying to 0.
   - **`cubic-bezier(0.30,0.60,0.50,1.00)`** (strong ease-out, rms 0.010). No overshoot; settle is 3 frames at ±0.2 %.
4. **3D turn and back-off**: car f158–f180, 22 f = 0.92 s.
   - The car turns about 120° to show its rear while sliding back 90.4 → 84.4 % W.
   - `cubic-bezier(0.65,0.40,0.35,0.80)` (≈ ease-in-out, rms 0.016).
   - In code: `rotateY` on a 2.5D card, or swap between 3–4 pre-rendered views with crossfades.
5. **Drive-out**: car f209–f241 (still moving at the end), front x 48.6 → 17.7 % W = **0.97 %W/frame (23 %W/s), almost linear** (`cubic-bezier(0.10,0,0.65,0.70)`, i.e. linear with a small ease-in). Car height 24 → 30 % H.
6. **Ghost dissolve-in**: classical arch f148–f170, 22 f = 0.92 s.
   - Luminance std in the arch box: 2.5 → 67.9, measured per frame. Visible from f150 at about 4 % opacity; full by f163.
   - **`cubic-bezier(0.55,0.30,0.55,1.10)`** (ease-in-out with a tiny 1–2 % overshoot; rms 0.012). The core ramp f150–f163 (13 f) is close to linear at about 7.5 %/frame.
   - The arch arrives slightly blurred/ghosted and sharpens as it gains opacity (a soft-focus fade).
7. **Ghost dissolve-out**: traffic light f155–f163, 8 f = 0.33 s. f155 solid, f159 about 40 % opacity, f163 gone. Linear opacity.
8. **Gradient melt**: red triangle f121–f241, 120 f = 5 s.
   - Red area 0.47 → 0 % of the frame. The base fades to transparent from the bottom up while the apex stays solid, ending as a small red "cone" at f229–f241.
   - `cubic-bezier(0.30,0,0,0.40)` (rms 0.016). That is 0 % change for the first 12 f, then a fast first half (about 60 % gone by f175), then a slow tail.
9. **Morph recolour**: beach ball red stripe → teal f60–f76, 16 f = 0.67 s. Red area 1.84 → 0.15 %. About linear (rms 0.083 linear, 0.027 fitted `cubic-bezier(0.95,0.90,0.40,0.80)`). Build it as a crossfade between two colourways of the same cut-out.
10. **Jump swap (1-frame pop-out)**: f112 → f113. Pulcinella disappears completely in one frame (region diff 27.2 vs 6–11 neighbours); the triangle and paper note behind are instantly revealed. f196 → f199 is the same kind of event for the peacock: blue area 1.33 (f196) → 1.16 → 1.00 → 0 (f199), after a slow 10-frame shrink 1.75 → 1.33. Fit `cubic-bezier(0.90,0.20,1.00,0.30)` = hard ease-in, snap at the end.
11. **Draw-on grow**: orange chevron f180–f198, 18 f = 0.75 s.
    - Right extent 68.2 → 83.6 % W; arm tip rises to y ≈ 42 % H.
    - f181 jumps 5.4 % in one frame (appear-pop at 35 % progress), then about 0.6 %W/frame linear, easing out over the last 4 frames.
    - Fit `cubic-bezier(0,0.30,0.05,0.50)` with an initial jump, so model it as `opacity 0→1 in 1 f` + `scaleY 0.35→1` linear over 16 f + 4 f ease-out.
    - Tip over-extension ≤ 0.3 % W (83.6 vs 83.3), i.e. effectively no overshoot.
12. **Camera-coupled drift of static shapes**: the disc, triangle and paper never move on their own. They drift only because of the push (triangle cx 54.1 → 57.2 % from f1–f61 = 0.05 %W/frame, outward from the anchor).

Stagger: events overlap rather than queue. There is always 1 major object action plus the camera push, and a new beat starts every 0.7–1.5 s (f60, f113, f137, f148, f158, f180, f196, f209).

## 9. Transitions
No scene transitions: one continuous shot with no cuts. In-shot element transitions, frame by frame:
- **Jump swap, f112 → f113:**
  - f112: Pulcinella full, holding the note in front of the triangle.
  - f113: figure gone; triangle (full red, 6.4 % W) and parchment note fully present.
  - f114 onward: steady.
  - Ball shading also jumps (different stripe layout).
- **Cross-dissolve swap, f148–f164** (two objects overlap in time):
  - f148: arch at 0 % opacity, traffic light 100 %.
  - f150: arch about 4 %.
  - f155: arch about 30 %, light still 100 %.
  - f157: arch about 45 %, light about 70 % and lightening (dark-pixel count 12 % → 0 between f155 and f157).
  - f159: arch about 60 %, light about 40 % ghost.
  - f163: arch about 95 %, light 0 %.
  - f164–f170: arch sharpens to 100 %.
- **Morph-replace, f196–f199 (peacock → column stump):**
  - f195: peacock 100 %.
  - f197: body becomes a translucent blue urn shape at about 60 %. The motion spike of 3.24 comes from this plus the chevron.
  - f198: about 40 %, a small teal ball pops in at (58 %, 77 %).
  - f199: peacock gone, a short grey column stump stands in its place.
- **Shape melt, f163–f241:** the triangle's lower half fades to paper through a vertical gradient (§8 M8).

Classification: hard in-place swap (1 f), cross-dissolve (8–16 f), morph (3–4 f), gradient melt (5 s). None of them are wipes, pushes or masks.

## 10. Camera
- **One push-in only.** Total ×1.43 over 10 s. Speeds: about 0 %/s for f1–f49; **6–7 % scale/s for f61–f169**; 3 %/s for f169–f241. The anchor sits left of and below centre, at about (29 %, 54 %).
- **No pan** beyond what the off-centre anchor implies: a point at the frame centre drifts about +0.07 %W/frame right and −0.04 %H/frame up.
- **No roll, no shake** (measured rotation |r| < 0.5° and not systematic).
- **Pseudo-parallax / re-staging:** objects scale independently of the camera, so it reads as 2.5D. Colosseum width about 12 % W (f1) → about 28 % W (f241), ×2.3 vs camera ×1.43. Car ×1.2 by f43. Static flat shapes scale at about the camera rate. So the back architecture grows *faster* than the camera; that comes from generative re-staging, not physical parallax.

<AbsoluteFill style={{transform: `scale(${scale})`, transformOrigin: '29% 54%'}}>{/* cluster */}</AbsoluteFill>

Scale the source timing by duration: the push uses the whole clip length.

## 12. Signature details and anti-patterns
MUST:
- A single compact collage island on a big empty warm page (paper ≥ 73 % of the frame, cluster ≤ 70 % W). The paper is flat `#ece9e0`, not textured kraft.
- Mix museum-photo cut-outs (stone in sepia-grey, objects in muted colour) with flat geometric paper shapes in **only** ochre `#c9913a`, brick red `#b82717` and teal `#47707f`.
- Hairline ink connectors with open circles and dots, plus parchment scraps with illegible cursive.
- A soft feathered ground patch (cobbles) and contact shadows, so objects stand on something.
- Smooth 24 fps motion plus a never-settling slow push-in (×1.4 over 10 s, off-centre anchor).
- Constant small story beats: every 0.7–1.5 s one object moves, turns, appears or dissolves. Several changes overlap.

ANTI-PATTERNS:
- Stepped on-2s/on-3s timing, wiggle boil or jitter. This reference is fully smooth.
- White keylines or stickers around cut-outs, hard drop shadows, or bouncy overshoot springs (measured overshoot ≤ 2 %).
- Hard cuts, wipes or whip pans.
- Pure white (`#fff`), pure black or saturated primaries beyond the three accents.
- Big type. There is none.
- Filling the frame edge to edge, or centring everything symmetrically (the mass sits slightly left and the push anchor is left-low).
- Trying to reproduce the AI morphs literally (peacock → column, 3D turns of flat cut-outs) with tweens. Use crossfades between pre-made states, or 2.5D card rotation, instead.

- **Measured (medium):**

  - Fixed-point location ±5 %.
  - Car drive-out (5 manual reads).

  - The odd/even diff alternation is attributed to interpolation or the encoder, not confirmed.
