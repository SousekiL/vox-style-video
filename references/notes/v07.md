## 1. Summary
A vertical mixed-media "then and now" collage. The base is a hand-tinted sepia photo of a baroque city hall with a clock tower, glued between two plaster-paper fields: an off-white sky above, a dusty ground with an ochre paint stripe below. A dark asphalt road strip runs between them, edged with cut-out rocks and grass.

A greyscale horse-drawn carriage drives in, parks and drives off. A saturated red 1950s car then glides in and parks in the same slot. Two columns of bold Malayalam captions plus a big italic-serif "1000" flank the clock tower. The look depends on three things:
- **Colour encodes era**: the old vehicle is greyscale, the new one is the only saturated thing in the frame.
- **Clean, soft AE motion**: text fades, blurs and rises in; the car moves on a long easy-ease. This is *not* jittery stop-motion.
- **One stepped layer**: the carriage moves at **12 fps** while everything else moves at 30 fps.

## 7. Textures and materials
- **Sky paper**: grey-beige plaster/parchment with low-frequency blotches (σ of luminance ≈ 6–8 levels on 255) and faint darker stains near the top-left.
  - Code: a flat #e6dfd8 fill.
  - Add `feTurbulence type="fractalNoise" baseFrequency="0.006" numOctaves="3"` through `feColorMatrix` to ±4% luminance, multiplied in.
  - Add a second, finer noise (baseFrequency 0.8) at 3–5% opacity for tooth.
- **Ground paper**: the same plaster but darker (#cdc2ba), with sparse dark specks 1–2 px.
  - Ochre stripe: a 9–12% W wide curved band with feathered edges (≈2–3% W soft falloff), like dry paint. It curves from x 70% (top of ground) to x 80% (bottom).
  - Code: an SVG `path` stroke with `filter: blur(6px)` at @1080, `mix-blend-mode: multiply`, opacity 0.55.
- **Building photo**: sepia hand-tinted archival photo (warm browns, with the facade's crisp detail retained).
  - Cut out along the skyline with a **cream feathered halo**: ≈3–5 px rect = **1–1.4% W** (≈10–15 px @1080), colour #f6f7e4, soft outer edge. This reads like a sticker/white cut border that has been blurred.
  - Code: `feMorphology operator="dilate" radius="8"` on SourceAlpha, then `feGaussianBlur stdDeviation="3"`, then `feFlood #f6f7e4` + `feComposite in`, placed behind the image.
- **Torn paper strip**: a single cream-white jagged rip running from the tower base (x ≈ 57% W, y 33% H) down to x ≈ 47% W, y 63% H.
  - About **2–3% W** wide, ~30% H long, tilted ≈15° from vertical, with ragged saw-tooth edges (period ≈1% H).
  - It reads as a tear in the photo showing the white paper core.
  - Code: a polygon with jitter on both edges (random offset 0.3–0.8% W every 0.8–1.2% H), fill #f6f7e4, and a 1 px darker inner edge at 30% opacity.
- **Vehicles**: photographic cut-outs with clean, slightly soft mattes. No outline, no drop shadow, no contact shadow (wheels just sit on the road line). The carriage and horse are greyscale. The car is full colour and glossy.
- **Rocks/grass**: photo cut-outs (rocks greyscale-beige, grass desaturated green), no outline.
- **Grain**: an adjustment layer named "Grain PRE" exists. Grain size cannot be measured through the phone capture (moiré and noise dominate). Use fine monochrome grain, 1 px @1080, at 4–6% opacity, regenerated every frame.
- **Not present**: halftone, scribbles, tape, stamps, light leaks, vignette (I measured no edge darkening in the rectified comp beyond the capture fall-off).

## 8. Motion vocabulary
1. **Stepped slide-stop (vintage vehicle)**: the carriage and horse move together as one layer.
   - Motion: +100 px rect = **+27.8% W** over **f25–f50 = 25 frames (0.83 s)**, ease-out.
   - Progress per held step: 0, .24, .36, .42, .54, .59, .71, .82, .87, .99, 1.
   - Approximate easing: `cubic-bezier(0.2, 0.5, 0.4, 1)`. No overshoot.
   - **Stepped at 12 fps** (hold pattern 2,3,2,3), so the position only updates every 2.5 frames.
   - Measured f25–f50 and repeated identically at f318–f345.
2. **Stepped ease-in-out exit**: the same layer leaves to the right.
   - Offsets from rest (rect px): f192 +3, f195 +7, f197 +16, f200 +33, f203 +64, f205 +106, f207 +148, f210 +181, f212 +207, f215 +226, f217 +243.
   - Peak speed ≈21 px/frame = **5.8% W per frame** around f205.
   - It then decelerates and creeps along the right edge, ~half off-frame, until the layer is cut at ≈f238.
   - Total ≈46 frames (1.5 s), 12 fps stepped. The car's entry overlaps this exit by 13 frames (f225–f238), so the two vehicles cross like a relay.
   - Some intermediate frames show a double image. This is most likely the phone exposure spanning a monitor refresh, not frame blending in the design.
3. **Long-glide park (modern vehicle)**: the red car, at 30 fps.
   - Car front x (rect px), f226 onward: 12, 23, 39, 55, 79, 103, 126, 147, 166, 184, 200, 213, 225, 235, 244, 252, 261, 266, 274, 277, 284, 287, 293, 298, 301, 303, 309, 310, 311, 316, 318, 319, 323, 324, 326, 326, 329 (f262), then 330 by f311.
   - Best fit: **duration 48 frames (1.6 s), `cubic-bezier(0.25, 0.4, 0.1, 1)`**, RMS error 0.9%. Total travel: left edge from −82.8% W to +8.6% W, i.e. **91.4% W**.
   - Speed: fast acceleration in ≈6 frames to a peak of 24 px/frame = **6.7% W per frame (200% W/s)** at f230–232, then a long ease-out tail. No overshoot or bounce.
   - This matches the AE easy-ease keyframe pair at ≈6.7 s → 8.2 s (45 frames) visible in the timeline.
   - **Motion blur** is on (the layer shows a "Motion Blur switch set" tooltip). The wheels and body smear visibly over f229–f236. At a 180° shutter the blur is ≈0.5 × velocity ≈ 3.3% W at peak.
4. **Line reveal (rise + fade + unblur)**: described in §5. 6–7 frames visible, ease-out, rise 1.9% H, blur ≈10 px @1080 → 0, opacity 0→1. Stagger 4–6 frames between lines and 22–29 frames between columns. Smooth 30 fps. Measured on "1000" f28–f34, f323–f331, and on lines f50–f61 and f239–f280.
5. **Line exit (fall + fade + blur)**: 3–4 frames per line, ease-in, fall 1.6% H. Reverse order, 1–2 frame stagger inside a block, ≈8–10 frames between blocks (f175–f190).
6. **Hold (no boil)**: during the holds (f62–f174, f262–f316) the parked carriage stays within ±1 px rect (σ = 1.0 px x, 1.06 px y; `bob.csv`), which is the tracking noise floor. The building, paper, rocks and text never jitter. **No wiggle or boil anywhere.**

## 9. Transitions
There are no scene transitions inside the comp. The "scene change" happens **in place**: the vehicles swap on the same road while the background stays static.
- f175–f190: the old captions leave (§8.5).
- f192: the carriage starts to exit right.
- f225: the car's nose enters at the left edge (overlap 13 frames).
- f239: new captions start, 14 frames after the car enters and **while the car is still decelerating**, so text and vehicle motion overlap.
- f268: the second column arrives.
- The comp ends on a hold and **hard-cuts back to frame 0** (f317→f318). f317 is a camera double exposure (car and carriage both at ~50% opacity). Treat it as a hard cut, not a dissolve.
- The project name "...Linked Comp 09" (with a "Linked Comp 10" tab) suggests that consecutive comps are chained. The carriage already being 70% on-screen at frame 0 means it carries over from the previous comp. Treat a scene boundary as a **continuous object hand-off**, not a cut.

## 10. Camera
- **Inside the comp: no camera move at all**. Building position is constant within ±1 px rect across the whole loop. There is no zoom, no parallax and no shake. All life comes from the layer animation.
- Capture artefacts (ignore when rebuilding):
  - The handheld phone drifts the panel left by 54 px source (≈1.5% W/s) over f1–f145.
  - Over f289–f384 the phone pans right by 49% of source width in 95 frames (≈15% W/s), zooms in about 12% and auto-exposure blows out the comp to near-white.

## 12. Signature details and anti-patterns
**Must have**
- Sepia archival architecture photo with a **cream feathered cut-out halo** and a **white torn strip** through it.
- Plaster-paper sky and ground with no hard graphic shapes.
- The past is greyscale and the present is the only saturated object (red car about 6% of the frame).
- Text in two columns that frame a central vertical landmark, set in a **y 12–25% H** band above the roofline.
- Mixed type: italic Didone numeral plus heavy rounded display lines in 3 sizes.
- Line-by-line **fade + unblur + 2% H rise**, 6–8 frames, 4-frame stagger. Exits are faster and run in reverse.
- The vintage object moves **posterized to 12 fps** while text and the modern object move smoothly at 30 fps with motion blur. The contrast in cadence is part of the period story.
- Long, calm easy-ease (1.5 s for a 90% W move) and long holds of 3.5 s or more. The scene changes **in place** (object relay), not by cutting.

**Breaks the look**
- Boil/wiggle on still elements or a jittery camera (this piece is perfectly still).
- Overshoot or bounce on the vehicles or text.
- Per-letter typewriter text, or text on labels or tape.
- Drop shadows under the vehicles.
- More than one saturated colour.
- Pure #000 text or pure #fff paper.
- Whip pans, zooms or scene cuts between beats.
- Text overlapping the photo or landmark.

## 13. Confidence
**Crop caveat**: the source is a phone video of a monitor, not a screen recording. I located the comp panel with SIFT homographies of the monitor plane against frame 1 (corners measured by hand on 4× zooms), then perspective-rectified it to 360×640.
- Registration is stable to about ±1–2 px rect for f1–f305.
- After f320 the panel runs off the right of the phone frame and is over-exposed, so f320–f384 is only usable for timing.
- Rect resolution is ≈1/3 of the comp, with moiré, phone noise and an exposure/white-balance shift. Hex values are therefore approximate: measured values are given, plus a ×1.37 exposure correction that is a guess.
- Grain, fine texture and edge widths (halo, torn strip) are estimates to ±50%.

**Measured** (per frame, scripts in `work/`):
- Loop length (293 frames).
- 12 fps stepping of the carriage (hold pattern and diff cadence).
- Carriage entry and exit positions.
- Car trajectory and bezier fit (RMS 0.9%).
- Text appearance and disappearance frames, ink strength, sharpness, and the rise distance (centroid).
- Line staggers.
- No boil (σ ≈ 1 px).
- Layout bands, text sizes and centring.
- Red area share.

**Inferred or guessed**:
- Comp size of 1080×1920 (from the 9:16 aspect and the "vertical" workspace).
- Blur radius in px (from the sharpness ratio).
- The carriage easing beziers (fitted by eye to held steps).
- Exit easing type.
- Font matches.
- Whether the 12 fps comes from a 12 fps precomp or Posterize Time (the result is the same).
- Whether the double images on stepped frames are capture artefacts (most likely) or AE frame blending.
- Keyframe times read off the blurry AE timeline (±0.1 s).
